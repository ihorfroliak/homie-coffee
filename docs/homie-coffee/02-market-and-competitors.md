# 02 — Market and competitors (Gate 0 scope)

**Scope note.** This is the focused competitor and market research the Commercial Feasibility
Gate needs — not the full market chapter. No SEO roadmap, no keyword architecture, no content
plan. Those are deliberately out of scope until the gate is passed (D-012).

**Evidence base.** Six research streams run 2026-08-20/21. Every number below carries a source
key into [`sources.md`](sources.md). Claims are tagged **FACT** (read off a cited page),
**ESTIMATE** (derived or averaged), or **INFERENCE** (reasoned, not stated by any source).

---

## The answer first

Three findings decide this gate, and two of them are bad.

1. **The price corridor is externally set and a new brand cannot escape it.** Verified Polish
   accessible-speciality retail runs **120–190 PLN/kg**; premium runs 188–276 PLN/kg; Allegro
   sells 1 kg bags labelled "specialty" at **59.98–119.99 PLN/kg**. A new entrant realistically
   prices 120–180 PLN/kg and cannot win on price.
2. **The only publicly quoted Polish private-label cost is 124 PLN/kg** — sitting *inside* the
   retail band it would have to be sold above. If that number is representative, the gross margin
   on accessible speciality is far too thin to absorb Polish paid-acquisition cost.
3. **Median Polish Google Ads CPA is 69.30 PLN (Performance Max) / 86.46 PLN (Search)** against a
   small-shop AOV of 187 PLN and a 1.4% conversion rate. **Paid acquisition costs roughly the
   entire first-order gross profit.** This is a repeat-purchase business or it is nothing.

The one genuinely encouraging finding: **the private-label barrier in Poland is artwork, not
volume.** One roaster will produce from 20 units, and with client-supplied labels, from none at
all. Entry cost is hundreds of PLN, not tens of thousands.

## 1. The supply side — can a brand with no history get its own label on a bag?

**Yes, and cheaply.** This was the biggest positive surprise of the research.

| Roaster | MOQ | Published price | Packaging | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Lafaye** (Warsaw) | **20 units per SKU; no minimum at all with client-supplied labels** | none published | client's or theirs; label design 150 PLN (template) / 350 PLN (custom) | SUP-01, SUP-02 |
| **Palarnia Meksyk** (Rybnik) | not published | **31 PLN / 250 g = 124 PLN/kg**, 59 PLN / 500 g = 118 PLN/kg — explicitly "uśrednione kwoty" | supplied: black doypack, zip + degassing valve; template label edits free | SUP-05, SUP-06 |
| **Roastains** | 50 packages one-off; **10 packages on an annual contract** | none published | label max 90 × 120 mm | SUP-11 |
| **Coffee Hunter** | 16 kg single origin / 20 kg blends | none published | own or client's; print 0.20 PLN/bag/colour, full print needs several thousand units | SUP-09, SUP-10 |
| **Blueberry Roasters** | 80 kg | none published | — | SUP-13 |
| **Quba Caffe** (Łódź) | 500 pieces per design | **from 2,900 PLN**, plus 1,500–2,000 PLN net for three design concepts | full service incl. packaging production | SUP-07, SUP-08 |
| **Maestro Cafe** | 0 units with stickers; **10,000 units** for flexoprint | none published | — | SUP-14 |

Six further roasters publish nothing at all (Pan Kawosz, Tommy Cafe, Noble Coffee, J.J. Darboven
Poland, SCO, Caffedelmondo).

**INFERENCE — minimum cash to put our own label on a bag: under ~850 PLN** via Lafaye (20 × 250 g
plus a 150 PLN label), or ~4,400–4,900 PLN net via Quba Caffe at 500 pieces. **This fits inside
even the 2,000 PLN lean validation ceiling** (A2). Supply access is not the blocker.

### The blocker is that nobody publishes a cost per kilogram

**Zero of eight wholesalers publish PLN/kg.** Segafredo shows only "35% discount after login";
Coffeedesk, Tommy Cafe, The White Bear, CoffeeLand and Kawa dla Poznania publish nothing.
The only wholesaler with concrete public terms is **Hurtownia Kawy i Herbaty** — 300 PLN net
minimum order, volume discount up to 12% (headline "up to 20%"), 19.80 PLN net shipping to 27 kg
(SUP-21).

So the single most important number in the entire financial model — **cost per kg** — rests on
**one** publicly quoted figure (Palarnia Meksyk, 124 PLN/kg) that the source itself calls an
average. **This is the critical missing evidence of the whole gate** (SUP-32). It can only be closed by
quotation; the RFQ is drafted and unsent ([outreach template](model/supplier-outreach-template.md),
D-015).

### Resale and dropshipping are weaker than they look

- **Resale does not build a brand.** No wholesale programme found permits a reseller to relabel;
  rebranding is sold separately as private label (SUP-35, INFERENCE). Route B means selling
  *someone else's* brand.
- **Dropshipping has no evidential basis at all.** No Polish coffee roaster or wholesaler
  publishes dropshipping terms on its own site; only third-party aggregator blogs name candidates
  without primary confirmation (SUP-33). It cannot be modelled and must not be assumed.

## 2. The demand side — price bands and the free-delivery threshold

Eleven Polish stores verified as trading on 2026-08-20.

### Price per kg, three clean tiers

| Tier | Range | Verified examples |
|---|---|---|
| Craft / commodity floor | **79–120 PLN/kg** | NUNO Classic Blend 81.36 PLN/kg (30% arabica / 70% robusta), single origins 79.44–88.08 PLN/kg |
| **Accessible speciality** — our target band | **120–190 PLN/kg** | Coffee & Sons 119.97–151.60 · Świeżo Palona 145–159.60 · Figa 140–172 · BeMyBean 156–176 · JAVA 170–190 |
| Premium speciality | **188–276 PLN/kg** | COFFEE PLANT 188–248 · HAYB 276 direct (their own page prints "250g (276.00PLN/kg)") |

Two structural regularities, both **FACT**, both directly usable:

- **Blends undercut single origins by ~20–30% per kg** at every shop checked.
- **1 kg packs undercut 250 g by 9–28% per kg.** Coffee & Sons states it outright: 14% cheaper
  per kg at 1 kg, 21% at 3 × 1 kg. Figa: 43 PLN/250 g (172/kg) vs 140 PLN/1 kg (140/kg).

### The free-delivery threshold — the most commercially important number found

Thresholds observed: 49, 99*, 100, 100, 129, 149, 149, 149, 150, 159, 199, 199, 200, 200, 249 PLN,
plus one roaster (Figa) with **no threshold at all** (flat 10 PLN locker / 18 PLN courier).
*\*HAYB's reduced in-subscription threshold.*

- **Median and modal consumer threshold: 149 PLN.** Working band **129–159 PLN**.
- **Courier thresholds sit 50–100 PLN above locker thresholds** at the same shop (COFFEE PLANT
  100/200, Etno Cafe 199/249, BeMyBean 49/150).

**INFERENCE with direct consequences for the offer:** a 149 PLN threshold against an accessible-
speciality price of ~160 PLN/kg means the customer must buy roughly **1 kg, or three 250 g bags**,
to reach free delivery. The market has already engineered its baskets away from the single bag.
Any offer built around a single 250 g purchase is fighting both the market norm and the shipping
arithmetic (§4).

### Subscriptions — do NOT assume a recurring-billing engine is the norm

Of five subscription offers examined, only **two** run true card-on-file recurring billing:

| Shop | Mechanism | Terms |
|---|---|---|
| **HAYB** | PayU Płatność Cykliczna, **the only payment option** | 1 PLN card-verification charge; debit on the 1st, 00:00–06:00; retries +24 h and +48 h; **10% off at start, permanent 15% from the 3rd renewal**; free delivery from **99 PLN** vs 159 PLN normally |
| **JAVA** | saved card, charged on the 1st | 115 PLN per 30-day cycle for 2 × 250 g or 2 × 500 g; free shipping; pause/skip/cancel anytime |
| Krakowska Palarnia Kawy | **prepaid 6- or 12-month block**, min 6 months | 156–336 PLN gross; currently out of stock |
| NUNO | **manual — phone/e-mail**, no online pricing or mechanics at all | — |
| Coffeedesk | card-on-file described only in a **2021-dated** blog post; not surfaced on the live 2026 brand page | — |

**This is the most important strategic finding for MVP scope.** Roughly half the market has *not*
built recurring billing, and the largest player's subscription is not visibly live. Ihor's
instruction to downgrade the subscription engine (A9) is **supported by the evidence** — a
waitlist, a prepaid block or a manual pilot is a legitimate market-normal mechanism here, not a
compromise. Where a discount is published it is **10–15%**, and **free shipping is the more
consistently offered subscription incentive than a percentage discount**.

### Discovery sets are the standard acquisition weapon

- Coffee & Sons **Zestaw startowy**: 4 × 250 g for **139 PLN** (139 PLN/kg), and it carries **free
  delivery regardless of the 149 PLN threshold** — the whole homepage leads with it.
- HAYB Starter Packs: **111–217 PLN at 15–20% off**.
- Coffee & Sons 4 × 1 kg discovery at a stated **99.75 PLN/kg** ("28% taniej").

**INFERENCE:** the market's answer to "how do I get a first order above the shipping threshold" is
a discounted multi-bag discovery set with shipping folded in. That is the acquisition offer to
copy in structure — the question the gate must answer is whether it can be afforded (§4, doc 05).

### B2B is universally gated

Every B2B offer found hides prices behind a login or a contact form. The two published data
points: **COFFEE PLANT — no minimum order, free B2B delivery above 400 PLN net**; HAYB's B2B sits
entirely behind a login. Public B2B price discovery in Polish coffee is effectively impossible
without registering a business account.

## 3. Market size and direction

| Metric | Value | Tag | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Polish coffee retail market | **7.4 bn PLN (2024) → 7.7 bn (2025F)**, +5.1% y/y | FACT | BM-02 |
| Value vs volume | value **+13%**, average price **+15%**, **volume −2%** (Oct 2024–Sep 2025) | FACT | BM-03 |
| Only volume-positive formats | **whole bean +7%**, capsules +8%; ground, instant and mixes all declining | FACT | BM-04 |
| E-commerce channel growth | **+17%**, fastest-growing channel; ~50% of purchases still in discounters | FACT | BM-05 |
| Speciality sub-segment | **~140 m PLN/yr, ~2% of the Polish coffee market** (vs ~3% globally); ~300 speciality cafés | FACT | BM-09 |
| Retail coffee inflation | ground **+30.1% in 2025**, instant +15.7%; 2026 forecast +3–7% | FACT | BM-29 |
| Arabica futures | **328.25 USc/lb** (2026-08-20), −12.52% y/y, still ~25% below the Feb 2025 ATH of 440.85 | FACT | BM-26 |
| Green C-market translated | **~26.70 PLN/kg** before differential, freight, duty and roast weight loss | INFERENCE | BM-28 |

**Read this honestly.** The directional signals are favourable — whole bean is the only growing
format, e-commerce is the fastest-growing channel, and commodity pressure is easing. But **the
market is growing on price, not volume**, consumers have just absorbed a 30% ground-coffee price
rise and are price-fatigued, and **the speciality segment we would enter is ~140 m PLN — about 2%
of Polish coffee**. This is a small pond with, by the same research, roughly 300 speciality cafés
and at least eleven established DTC roasters already in it.

⚠ **Contested evidence:** speciality segment size is quoted at ~140 m PLN in one source and
~260 m PLN in another — an 86% discrepancy that was not reconcilable. Per-capita consumption is
quoted at 2.0, 2.5, 3.0 and 3.66 kg/person/year across sources, with no GUS primary found. Any
bottom-up TAM built on these is unreliable and none is built here.

## 4. What acquisition actually costs — the number that governs the gate

From a benchmark of **4,976 Polish campaigns across 114 accounts** (18 Jul 2025 – 17 Jul 2026;
286.9 m impressions, 8.53 m clicks, 518,302 conversions):

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| **Median CPA, Performance Max** | **69.30 PLN** |
| **Median CPA, Search** | **86.46 PLN** |
| Weighted Shopping CPA | 86.27 PLN |
| Weighted average CPC | 0.77 PLN overall · **1.58 PLN Search** · 0.64 PLN Shopping |
| Polish small-shop conversion rate (<100k PLN/mo) | **1.4%** |
| Polish small-shop AOV | **187 PLN** |
| Small-shop cart abandonment | 74% |

⚠ **Caveat that cuts both ways:** the AOV/CVR benchmark sample covers fashion, electronics, home &
garden, beauty and sports — **no food category**. Food typically converts *higher* than fashion
because it is a routine repeat purchase, so 1.4% may understate coffee. It is the best named
Polish benchmark found and it is not coffee-specific. Flagged as a Gate 0 evidence gap.

**Repeat purchase — the hinge, and the evidence is discouraging.** e-Izba / Mobile Institute
(Oct 2025, n=1,641): 78% of Polish consumers have preferred shops they return to, but **only ~11%
qualify as genuinely loyal, and 34% buy a brand only during promotions** (BM-16).

## 5. Marketplaces — cheaper than ads, but they cap the price

| Channel | Official fee | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Allegro** Supermarket category | **5% – 10.5% commission**, free listing; Allegro's own help article uses coffee at 50 PLN as its worked example at **5%** | BM-19, BM-20 |
| **Amazon.pl** grocery | **11% referral above 40 PLN** (5% below), min 1 PLN, plus **165.91 PLN/month** net Professional plan | BM-22, BM-23 |

**INFERENCE:** a 5–11% marketplace commission is dramatically cheaper than a 69–86 PLN CPA on a
~150 PLN order (46–58% of revenue). Marketplaces are the cheaper *acquisition* channel by a wide
margin. The cost is that they anchor the price: Allegro 1 kg "specialty" at 59.98–119.99 PLN/kg
and Amazon.pl mainstream 1 kg at 56.99–105.77 PLN/kg sit **below** the whole accessible-speciality
DTC band, and they take the customer relationship — which is precisely the asset a repeat-purchase
business needs. Not resolved here; it is a real strategic option to weigh at the next gate.

## 6. Competitors that could not be verified

Recorded so their absence is not mistaken for evidence:

- **Hard Beans** — hardbeans.pl redirects to hardbeans.com which returns 404. Repeatedly cited as
  a leading Polish speciality roastery; DTC status in 2026 unconfirmed.
- **Story Coffee** — HTTP 403 to the fetcher.
- **Audun Coffee** and **Coffee Proficiency** — both serve a TLS certificate covering only
  `*.home.pl`, so real customers would see a browser security warning. Commercially relevant in
  itself, but their pricing is unknown.
- **Etno Cafe** — prices read (36–42 PLN) but pack weight is not printed on the listing, so
  PLN/kg is not computable and it cannot be placed in a tier.

## 7. What this means for the gate

- **Supply access: not a blocker.** Entry is hundreds of PLN. ✅
- **Cost per kg: unknown, and it decides everything.** One public data point at 124 PLN/kg, inside
  the retail band it must be sold above. ⚠ **critical missing evidence**
- **Price ceiling: hard, externally set, 120–190 PLN/kg.** No pricing power for a new brand. ⚠
- **Basket must be ~1 kg or 3 bags** to clear the 149 PLN market shipping norm. ⚠
- **Paid acquisition at 69–86 PLN CPA cannot be paid for out of one order.** ⚠⚠
- **Subscription engine is not the market norm** — a waitlist or prepaid block is legitimate. ✅
- **Market growing on price, not volume; speciality is ~2% of a 7.7 bn PLN market.** ⚠
